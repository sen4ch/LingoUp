Every Saturday morning, Maria goes to the market with her grandmother. The market is in the old square, and it is always crowded. People come from the villages to sell fruit, vegetables, cheese and flowers.

Maria's grandmother knows everybody. She says hello to the man at the bread stall and asks about his children. Then she stops at the vegetable stall. "Look at the colour of these tomatoes," she tells Maria. "Red and shiny. That means they are fresh."

Maria wants to buy strawberries, but her grandmother says, "Wait. It's better to buy fruit at the end of the morning. The sellers don't want to take it home, so the prices are lower." At half past twelve, they go back to the fruit stall. Now two boxes of strawberries cost only three euros. What a bargain!

Maria pays with a five-euro note and the seller gives her the change. Their bags are heavy now, so Maria carries the strawberries and her grandmother carries the vegetables. On the way home, they eat a few strawberries. They are sweet and delicious.
