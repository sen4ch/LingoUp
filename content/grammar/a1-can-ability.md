## Meaning and use

Can is a modal verb used to express ability, possibility in a situation, or informal permission. Its form stays the same for every subject. The following verb is the base form without to. A can question moves can before the subject, and a negative uses cannot or can’t. What a can sentence means depends on context: Can you swim? may ask about ability, while Can I sit here? normally asks for permission.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Subject + can + base verb: She can swim.
- Negative: cannot or can’t + base verb.
- Question: Can + subject + base verb?
- Short answers: Yes, I can. No, I cannot.

## Examples in context

> My grandfather can use a smartphone.
> You cannot park in front of this gate.
> Can we borrow these chairs?

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not add third-person s: She can play, not she cans play. Do not add to after can: can help, not can to help. For a future ability, be able to is often needed: I will be able to drive next year. Can is not followed by another central modal: use may be able to, not may can.
