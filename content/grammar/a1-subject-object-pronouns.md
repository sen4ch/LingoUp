## Meaning and use

Personal pronouns replace noun phrases when the listener knows who or what you mean. **Subject pronouns** appear as the subject of a clause. **Object pronouns** normally follow a verb or preposition. You can refer to one person of unknown or unspecified gender with they and them; the verb still takes the usual they form. It is normally used for things and for animals when their sex or personal identity is not relevant. Avoid a pronoun if two possible people would make the meaning unclear.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Subjects: I, you, he, she, it, we, they.
- Objects: me, you, him, her, it, us, them.
- After a preposition: with me, for her, between us.
- Singular they still takes are: Someone is here; they are waiting.

## Examples in context

> Lena is my neighbour. She works with me.
> We like those songs. We listen to them every day.
> Someone left their keys. Can you help them?

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Always write **I** with a capital letter. Do not use a subject form after an ordinary preposition: for me, not for I. In a combined subject, use My friend and I are ready. In an object, use The guide helped my friend and me. You is both singular and plural, so context supplies the number.
