## Meaning and use

A third conditional imagines a different past condition and a corresponding past result. It often expresses regret, criticism, relief, or a counterfactual explanation. Both sides look back at a past situation, but the sentence is not a factual report that those events occurred. Would have gives a hypothetical result; could have or might have can express ability or a less certain possible outcome.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- If + had + past participle, would have + past participle.
- Negative: If she had not left, we would not have missed her.
- Alternative result modals: could have, might have.
- Formal inversion: Had I known, I would have acted.

## Examples in context

> If we had checked the forecast, we would have brought coats.
> She might have joined us if we had invited her.
> Had they left earlier, they could have avoided the queue.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not put would have in the ordinary conditional clause: If I had known, not If I would have known. The contraction 'd can mean had or would, so identify the following verb form. A third conditional differs from a mixed conditional in the time of its result. Avoid assuming that could have always describes an event that actually happened.
