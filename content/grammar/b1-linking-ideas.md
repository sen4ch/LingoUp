## Meaning and use

Different linking expressions need different grammatical structures. Because, although, and so that introduce clauses with their own subjects and verbs. Because of, despite, and in spite of are preposition expressions followed by a noun phrase or an -ing form. To and in order to can introduce a purpose infinitive. A linking adverb such as however connects ideas across sentences but does not itself join independent clauses in the same way as a conjunction.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Because + clause; because of + noun phrase.
- Although/even though + clause; despite/in spite of + noun or -ing.
- Purpose: to/in order to + base form; so that + clause.
- However and therefore require appropriate sentence punctuation.

## Examples in context

> Despite the rain, the match continued.
> Although it was raining, the match continued.
> We left early so that we could avoid the traffic.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not write despite of. Do not pair although and but as duplicate links for the same two clauses in standard edited writing. A semicolon or full stop can precede however between independent statements; a comma alone is normally insufficient. Because of the fact that is possible but often heavier than because.
