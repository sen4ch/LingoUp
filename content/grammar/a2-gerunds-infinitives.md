## Meaning and use

Some verbs are followed by another verb in the -ing form, while others take a to infinitive. These are lexical patterns: the first verb often determines the form of the next one. An -ing form can also act like a noun, and a to infinitive often expresses a purpose. A central modal takes a base form without to. Learning a verb together with its following pattern is more reliable than translating isolated words.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Enjoy, avoid, finish, mind + -ing.
- Want, hope, decide, plan, learn + to infinitive.
- Preposition + -ing: interested in learning, good at drawing.
- Purpose: I went outside to make a phone call.

## Examples in context

> I enjoy cooking for my friends.
> We decided to take the earlier train.
> She is interested in studying architecture.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not say enjoy to swim: enjoy swimming is the usual pattern. A preposition can look like infinitive to but behave differently: look forward to meeting you uses -ing. Some verbs accept both forms, sometimes with a change of meaning; compare the later verb-pattern lessons. Not all -ing words describe a continuous tense: Swimming is useful has a noun-like subject.
