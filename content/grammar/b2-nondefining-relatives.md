## Meaning and use

A non-defining relative clause adds supplementary information about something already identifiable. In writing, commas separate it from the main clause. Unlike a defining clause, it does not normally select a subset of possible people or things. Who refers to people, which to things or sometimes an entire preceding clause, and whose expresses a relationship. The punctuation reflects the intended meaning, not simply the length of the clause.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- My brother, who lives abroad, is visiting.
- The house, which was built in 1900, needs repair.
- Whole-clause reference: She won, which surprised everyone.
- Use commas, retain the relative word, and do not use that in the standard pattern.

## Examples in context

> Mina, whose sister is a doctor, works at the library.
> The train was cancelled, which delayed our journey.
> Our only printer, which we bought last year, is broken.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not omit an object relative in a non-defining clause. Compare My sisters who live abroad are visiting with My sisters, who live abroad, are visiting: the first can select only some sisters; the second presents the relative information as supplementary. A sentence may need two commas if the clause is in the middle.
