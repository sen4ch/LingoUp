## Meaning and use

Too describes a degree beyond what is wanted or possible; it is not simply a stronger version of very. Enough expresses sufficient quantity or degree. So modifies an adjective or adverb; such modifies a noun phrase. These patterns can explain a result or a limitation, often with a to infinitive or a that clause. Position matters because enough behaves differently before nouns and after adjectives.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Too + adjective/adverb: too heavy, too quickly.
- Adjective/adverb + enough: old enough, clearly enough.
- Enough + noun: enough time; too much/too many + noun.
- So + adjective; such + a/an + adjective + singular noun.

## Examples in context

> This suitcase is too heavy to lift.
> We have enough chairs for everyone.
> It was such a useful meeting that we stayed longer.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Say warm enough, not enough warm. Too tired to work means the tiredness prevents working; very tired does not necessarily imply that. With uncountable nouns use such useful advice, without a/an. A normal that result clause needs a subject and finite verb: so cold that the lake froze.
