## Meaning and use

Have or get an object plus a past participle often means arranging for someone else to perform an action. The subject is not necessarily the person who carries it out. Get is often more conversational. Have can also describe an unwelcome experience without suggesting that the subject arranged it. Related patterns name the person responsible, but they use different complement forms.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Have/get + object + past participle: have the roof repaired.
- Have + person + base verb: have the assistant call me.
- Get + person + to infinitive: get the assistant to call me.
- Unwelcome experience: She had her bag stolen.

## Examples in context

> We are having the kitchen painted next week.
> I got my laptop repaired at a local shop.
> She had her passport stolen while travelling.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not assume have something done always means a paid service or a voluntary arrangement. Distinguish I cut my hair from I had my hair cut. The tense is carried by have/get; the final participle does not become a past-simple verb. Make someone do and get someone to do also differ in the form following the person object.
