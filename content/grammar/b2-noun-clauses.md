## Meaning and use

A clause can fill a role often occupied by a noun phrase, such as subject, object, or complement. A that clause reports a proposition; a whether clause presents an alternative or uncertainty. What can introduce a fused relative meaning the thing or things that. Embedded interrogatives use statement order. The exact clause type and the governing verb determine which introductory word is possible.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Statement complement: I believe that she is right.
- Fused relative: What she said surprised me.
- Alternative: Whether we leave depends on the weather.
- Extraposition: It is clear that we need more time.

## Examples in context

> I understand what you mean.
> The question is whether we can afford it.
> It seems that the office has moved.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not add a second subject after a clause already functioning as subject: What he said was useful, not What he said it was useful. What is not normally a relative word after an expressed noun: the book that I read, not the book what I read in standard edited English. That can often be omitted in object complement clauses, but not indiscriminately in every position.
