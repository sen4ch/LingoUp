## Meaning and use

Countable nouns can refer to separate units and normally have singular and plural forms. Uncountable nouns present a substance, activity, or idea as a mass rather than individual units. The distinction affects articles, numbers, agreement, and quantity words. A noun can be countable in one meaning and uncountable in another. A chicken is an animal; chicken on a menu often means meat. Learn the intended meaning together with the noun’s grammatical pattern.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Singular countable: a chair; plural countable: three chairs.
- Uncountable: water, advice, information, furniture, luggage.
- Units for a mass: a glass of water, two pieces of advice.
- Uncountable subjects usually take singular agreement: This information is useful.

## Examples in context

> We need a table and four chairs.
> There is some rice in the cupboard.
> Can you give me two pieces of information?

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not write an advice or many informations in ordinary standard usage. Use some advice or a piece of information. Some restaurant expressions count portions: two coffees means two cups or servings. Different nouns can behave differently across meanings, so coffee can be both a substance and a serving. Do not assume a word is countable because the equivalent is countable in another language.
