## Meaning and use

Coordination links units with comparable grammatical roles. The coordinated elements can be words, phrases, or clauses, but their roles must fit the surrounding construction. Correlative markers such as both ... and and not only ... but also should introduce logically comparable material. Some structures share a subject, auxiliary, or a final constituent through ellipsis, provided the reader can recover the missing material without a misleading attachment.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Parallel objects: She values accuracy and clarity.
- Parallel complements: to plan carefully and to check thoroughly.
- Balanced contrast: not only efficient but also reliable.
- Shared subject: We opened the files and checked the totals.

## Examples in context

> The role involves drafting reports, checking figures, and answering queries.
> They wanted either to postpone the meeting or to move it online.
> Maya ordered, and Leo paid for, the replacement parts.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Parallelism is a guide to clear structure, not a demand that every coordinated phrase have identical length. Check what each conjunction actually joins. A missing preposition or required complement can make a superficially balanced list ungrammatical. Place either, both, and not only so their scope matches the intended alternatives. Do not let elegant symmetry replace a clear logical comparison.
