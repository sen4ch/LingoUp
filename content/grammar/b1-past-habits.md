## Meaning and use

Used to describes a past habit or state that is no longer true or is contrasted with the present. Would can describe repeated past actions once a past setting is clear, but normally does not replace used to for a past state. Be used to means be accustomed to; get used to describes the process of becoming accustomed. The to in these last two expressions is a preposition, not the marker of a to infinitive.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Used to + base verb: We used to live here.
- Questions/negatives commonly use did + use to.
- Would + base verb for repeated actions in a past narrative.
- Be/get used to + noun or -ing form.

## Examples in context

> I used to own a motorbike.
> Every summer, we would visit the coast.
> She is getting used to working at night.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Distinguish I used to drive from I am used to driving. The first describes a former habit; the second describes present familiarity. Do not normally use would to state an isolated former possession: I used to have a red bicycle is clearer. In careful exam writing, did not use to is a widely taught negative pattern.
