## Meaning and use

A modal followed by have and a past participle can assess a past situation. Must have makes a strong positive inference, cannot have or could not have can mark an impossible explanation, and might have or may have express uncertainty. Could have can describe a past possibility or an unrealised opportunity depending on context. These are assessments from a viewpoint, not alternative conjugations of the past simple.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Must have + participle: a strong past deduction.
- May/might/could have + participle: a possible past situation.
- Cannot/could not have + participle: a strong negative deduction.
- Modal + have been + -ing: an activity possibly in progress in the past.

## Examples in context

> The ground is wet. It must have rained overnight.
> She cannot have seen the email; it was sent to another address.
> They might have been waiting at the wrong platform.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not confuse must have left, an inference, with had to leave, a past obligation. Could have succeeded does not tell you by itself whether success occurred. Avoid of in perfect modal forms: should have and might have, not should of and might of. Some negative modal deductions vary across dialects, so interpret them in context.
