## Meaning and use

A future time clause often uses a present form even when its meaning is future. This applies after when, as soon as, before, after, until, and similar conjunctions. The main clause can still use will or another future expression. Use a present perfect when you want to emphasise completion before the next future event. Distinguish a time clause from an embedded question about when something will happen.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- I will call when I arrive.
- We will leave as soon as she is ready.
- Do not start until I give the signal.
- I will send the report after I have checked it.

## Examples in context

> Take your coat before you go outside.
> As soon as the gates open, we will join the queue.
> I do not know when the results will arrive.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

The usual no-will pattern applies to future time adjuncts, not every clause beginning with when. I wonder when he will arrive is an embedded question and allows will. Until refers to the continuation of a situation up to a point; by marks a deadline for completion. A comma normally separates an initial time clause from its main clause.
