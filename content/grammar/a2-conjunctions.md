## Meaning and use

Conjunctions connect words, phrases, and clauses. And adds information, but signals contrast, or presents alternatives, because introduces a reason, and so introduces a result. A coordinating conjunction can join two clauses that could stand independently. A subordinating conjunction creates a dependent clause that needs a main clause. Make the logical relationship clear before deciding which connector to use.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Addition: I called and she answered. Contrast: I called but she did not answer.
- Reason: We stayed home because it was snowing.
- Result: It was snowing, so we stayed home.
- An initial because clause normally takes a following comma and main clause.

## Examples in context

> I like the design, but the price is too high.
> Would you prefer tea or coffee?
> Because the road was closed, we took another route.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not normally pair because and so to connect the same two clauses: Because it rained, we stayed inside; or It rained, so we stayed inside. A because clause alone is often incomplete in formal writing, though it can answer Why? in conversation. Avoid joining two independent statements with only a comma.
