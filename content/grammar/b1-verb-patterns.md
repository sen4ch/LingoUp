## Meaning and use

A verb can determine both the form and the participants of the clause that follows it. Want and ask can take an object plus a to infinitive, while make and let use a bare infinitive after an object in active clauses. Some verbs, including help, allow both a bare and a to infinitive. These patterns explain why directly translating the same sequence from another language may fail.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Want/ask/tell + object + to infinitive.
- Make/let + object + base form in active clauses.
- Help + object + base form or to infinitive.
- Suggest + -ing or that clause, not ordinary object + to infinitive.

## Examples in context

> They asked me to wait outside.
> The film made us laugh.
> I suggest taking the earlier train.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Say I want you to stay, not I want that you stay in the usual personal request pattern. Tell normally needs an object before the to infinitive: tell someone to leave. Make changes in the passive: We were made to wait. Advise can take different patterns; learn the complete structure instead of assuming every reporting verb behaves like tell.
