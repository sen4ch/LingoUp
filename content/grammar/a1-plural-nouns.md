## Meaning and use

A singular noun refers to one item; a plural noun refers to more than one. Most countable nouns add **-s**, but spelling and pronunciation depend on the end of the word. Some common nouns have irregular plurals, and others keep the same form. A plural noun normally requires a plural verb when it is the subject. Some words, such as information, are normally uncountable and do not form ordinary plurals in their everyday meaning.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Most nouns: book/books; after s, sh, ch, x: box/boxes.
- Consonant + y: city/cities; vowel + y: key/keys.
- Common irregulars: child/children, person/people, man/men, woman/women, foot/feet, tooth/teeth, mouse/mice.
- Same singular and plural: sheep, deer; fish is common for multiple individual fish.

## Examples in context

> There are three buses outside.
> The children have clean shoes.
> One sheep is sleeping; two sheep are eating.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not use an apostrophe to make an ordinary plural: **two books**, not two book’s. Nouns ending in f or fe vary: knife/knives, but roof/roofs. Nouns ending in o also vary: potato/potatoes, but photo/photos. Learn these as vocabulary patterns rather than applying one rule to every word. People is the usual plural of person in everyday language.
