## Meaning and use

Use **there is/there are** to introduce the existence or presence of something. The noun phrase following be tells us what exists. Use it is when you identify or describe a thing that is already the topic. There in this pattern is a grammatical subject; it does not necessarily mean in that place. Questions reverse be and there, and negative statements put not after be. These patterns are especially useful for describing rooms, places, and available facilities.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- There is + singular or uncountable noun: There is a cafe. There is water.
- There are + plural noun: There are two cafes.
- Questions: Is there a lift? Are there any buses?
- Negatives: There is not a bank. There are not any seats.

## Examples in context

> There is a post office near our flat.
> There are some eggs in the fridge.
> Is there a doctor here? Yes, there is.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not use it is when introducing an unspecified item: There is a cat outside, not It is a cat outside. In careful writing, use there are before plural noun phrases. Conversational there’s with a plural is heard, but learn the agreement pattern first. Avoid double subjects: There is a park, not There it is a park when the intended meaning is existence.
