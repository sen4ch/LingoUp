## Meaning and use

Non-finite verb phrases can express aspect and voice even though they do not independently carry ordinary tense and agreement. A perfect infinitive presents a situation as earlier than a relevant viewpoint. A continuous infinitive presents an activity in progress. A passive infinitive backgrounds the agent. Similar distinctions appear with -ing clauses and perfect participles. The interpretation comes from the governing expression and the surrounding time frame.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Simple: to work; continuous: to be working; perfect: to have worked.
- Passive: to be chosen; perfect passive: to have been chosen.
- Perfect -ing: having finished; passive -ing: being interviewed.
- Negative: not to have seen; not having received.

## Examples in context

> She seems to have misunderstood the message.
> The team appears to be preparing a response.
> Having been warned, we avoided the narrow path.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not equate a perfect infinitive mechanically with past time in every sentence. I hope to have finished by Friday looks back from a future deadline. Be careful about the subject understood for an introductory participle clause. Some verbs reject patterns that another verb allows, so select the governing verb's complement first, then add aspect or voice if the meaning requires it.
