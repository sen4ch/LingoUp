## Meaning and use

Determiners can identify a noun phrase, distribute a statement across members, or specify a proportion of a group. Each highlights individuals; every typically considers all members separately within a set. Partitive of phrases identify a subset of an already specified group. Predeterminers such as all and both can combine with an article or possessive in ways that ordinary central determiners cannot. Quantifier scope can change a sentence's interpretation.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Each/every + singular countable noun; each of + plural group.
- Most students, but most of the students.
- All (of) my notes; both (of) these options.
- Another + singular noun; other + plural or uncountable noun; the other for a specified remainder.

## Examples in context

> Every room has its own key.
> Most of the information was already available.
> I have two copies; one is mine and the other is yours.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not insert of before an unspecified general noun: most people, not most of people. Another is written as one word and normally introduces one additional item; another two hours is an established quantity pattern. Not every student passed means some may have passed, while no student passed excludes everyone. Each and every are similar but not interchangeable in every construction.
