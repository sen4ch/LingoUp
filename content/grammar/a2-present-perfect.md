## Meaning and use

The present perfect connects an earlier situation to the present. It can describe life experience, a result that matters now, or a state continuing until now. The usual form is have/has plus a past participle. The present-perfect viewpoint does not locate an event in a finished past period such as yesterday. Been and gone also differ: someone who has gone away is normally still away, while someone who has been somewhere has returned.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Have/has + past participle: We have finished.
- Ever and never often ask or state experience without a finished time.
- For introduces duration; since introduces a starting point.
- Already, yet, just, so far and recently often appear with a present connection.

## Examples in context

> I have lost my key, so I cannot open the door.
> Have you ever travelled overnight by train?
> She has known the family for years.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not combine an ordinary present-perfect event with a finished time: I saw her yesterday, not I have seen her yesterday. Use the past participle rather than the past-simple form: has taken, not has took. Some American usage accepts the past simple with already, just, and yet more readily than typical British usage.
