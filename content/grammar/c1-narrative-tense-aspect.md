## Meaning and use

Narratives use tense and aspect to manage the reader's viewpoint. The past simple often advances the sequence, the past continuous supplies an ongoing background, and the past perfect moves to an earlier event. Past perfect continuous can emphasise activity or duration before a past viewpoint. Writers can also shift deliberately to a historical present, but an unexplained shift can confuse the chronology.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Foreground event: The door opened.
- Background activity: People were talking.
- Earlier event: Someone had left a bag behind.
- Earlier duration: They had been waiting for an hour.

## Examples in context

> I was locking the door when I realised I had left my phone inside.
> By the time the bus came, we had been standing in the rain for forty minutes.
> She entered, looked around, and smiled.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not move every verb into the past perfect after one earlier event; return to the main narrative time when the sequence becomes clear. A past form can also express politeness or hypothetical remoteness rather than chronological past. Ask which viewpoint the reader currently occupies before selecting a tense. Repeated past actions do not automatically require a continuous form.
