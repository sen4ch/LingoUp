## Meaning and use

Multiword verbs combine a verb with a particle, a preposition, or both. Their meanings can be literal or idiomatic. Some transitive phrasal verbs permit an object between the verb and particle; others do not. A pronoun object must come before the particle in a separable construction. Prepositional verbs normally keep the preposition before its object. Learn each expression with its meaning, object pattern, and a short example.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Separable: turn off the lamp / turn the lamp off; turn it off.
- Inseparable prepositional pattern: look after the children.
- No object: The plane took off.
- Three-part expression: put up with the noise.

## Examples in context

> Please turn your phone off.
> I am looking after my sister's dog.
> They ran out of coffee before lunchtime.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not say turn off it in the separable meaning. Do not assume every short word after a verb is a movable particle. Look after him does not become look him after. Some combinations have different meanings with different patterns: take off a coat versus a plane taking off. A learner's dictionary can show whether the object pattern is separable.
