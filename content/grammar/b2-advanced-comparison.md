## Meaning and use

Comparison can relate changing degrees, compare quantities, or express a growing trend. The more ... the more links two scales: a change in one is associated with a change in the other. Repeated comparatives describe continuing change. Use fewer with plural countable nouns and less with uncountable quantities as a reliable edited-writing baseline. As ... as can be modified to specify a degree of equality or difference.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- The + comparative ..., the + comparative ... .
- Hotter and hotter; more and more convenient.
- Far/much/a little + comparative; nearly/just/not quite as ... as.
- Twice as many books; twice as much time.

## Examples in context

> The longer we waited, the colder we became.
> The instructions are getting more and more complicated.
> This route takes far less time.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not use than after the second as in an equality comparison. Do not combine more with an -er form. Less is established with some measurements and fixed expressions, such as less than ten minutes, so the countability guideline is not an absolute ban. Comparable items should be logically parallel: compare the population of one city with that of another, not the city itself.
