## Meaning and use

Fronting places material before its ordinary position to establish a topic, create contrast, or connect to the previous sentence. It does not always require inversion. Object fronting can retain ordinary subject–verb order. Some locative and directional expressions allow full inversion with a following noun subject, especially in descriptive writing. These structures depend on information flow, verb type, and the kind of subject.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Object fronting: That explanation I can accept.
- Locative fronting without inversion: In this room, we keep the archives.
- Full inversion: At the end of the corridor stood a guard.
- Pronoun subjects usually retain order: Here it comes, not Here comes it.

## Examples in context

> This suggestion we rejected immediately.
> Beyond the trees lay a small lake.
> Here comes the bus, and here it is at last.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not apply negative-adverbial inversion to every fronted phrase. Full inversion generally suits intransitive location or motion patterns rather than an arbitrary transitive verb. A pronoun already identifies a familiar topic, which helps explain its different behaviour. Use fronting when the contrast or narrative flow makes the unusual order worthwhile.
