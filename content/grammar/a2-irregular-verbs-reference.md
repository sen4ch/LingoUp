## Meaning and use

Use this reference to check the base, past-simple, and past-participle forms of common verbs. The past form can stand as a finite past-simple verb; a past participle normally needs an auxiliary or belongs to a non-finite phrase. A slash records an established alternative or an agreement-dependent form. Some verbs have additional less frequent forms or meanings, so the list is a practical reference rather than a claim that every English irregular verb is included.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Read each entry as base — past simple — past participle.
- After did, a modal, or infinitival to, choose the base form.
- After perfect have, choose the past participle.
- Learn the meaning and an example alongside the forms.

## Examples in context

> She went home, but he has gone to work.
> I saw the first performance; I have seen three performances altogether.
> They wrote the report that was sent yesterday.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Read has the same spelling in its three forms, but the present base commonly sounds like reed and the past forms like red. Lie meaning recline is lie/lay/lain; lay meaning put something down is lay/laid/laid; lie meaning tell an untruth is regular lied/lied. Born is used for birth, while borne is used for carrying and several other meanings. Gotten is common in American acquisition/change meanings; have got for possession behaves differently.
