## Meaning and use

Adverbs describe how, when, where, or how often something happens, or modify an adjective or another adverb. Many manner adverbs end in -ly, but several common forms are irregular. Frequency adverbs such as often usually go before a main verb and after be. Longer manner phrases often go after the object. Position can change emphasis, so these are useful defaults rather than restrictions on every sentence.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Adjective to adverb: careful/carefully, happy/happily.
- Irregular or unchanged: good/well, fast/fast, hard/hard.
- Frequency: I often walk; she is usually early; I have never tried it.
- Manner after object: She opened the box carefully.

## Examples in context

> He speaks very clearly.
> We are rarely late for class.
> The team worked hard all afternoon.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Hard and hardly do not mean the same thing: worked hard means with effort; hardly worked means almost did not work. Fastly is not the standard adverb. With a linking verb, an adjective normally describes the subject: The food tastes good. Good and well can have additional meanings, including well for health, so read the complete context.
