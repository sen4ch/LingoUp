## Meaning and use

The verb before a complement determines which structures are available and how the participants are understood. A promise normally makes the promiser the understood performer of its infinitive; persuade makes the object the performer. Perception verbs can contrast a whole event, expressed with a bare infinitive, with an event viewed in progress, expressed with -ing. Other verbs select prepositional, finite, or infinitival complements.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Promise to do: the subject undertakes the action.
- Persuade someone to do: the object is the understood performer.
- See/hear + object + bare infinitive or -ing.
- Prevent someone from doing; accuse someone of doing.

## Examples in context

> I promised to send the files.
> We persuaded the owner to reopen the garden.
> I saw him cross the road; I saw him crossing the road.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not treat a person object after every verb as having the same role. Explain normally takes something to someone, not explain someone something in the usual standard pattern. A perception bare infinitive can present an event as a whole without proving that the observer watched every instant. In a passive perception pattern, to returns: He was seen to leave.
