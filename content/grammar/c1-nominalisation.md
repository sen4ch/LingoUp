## Meaning and use

Nominalisation presents an action, process, or quality as a noun phrase rather than a clause. It can connect ideas, package familiar information, or create a more formal style. A noun phrase may allow a compact chain of description, but excessive nominalisation can hide who acted and make a text difficult to follow. Decide whether a noun or verb expresses the relationship more clearly in the intended context.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Verb to noun: decide/decision, investigate/investigation.
- Adjective to noun: accurate/accuracy, available/availability.
- Clause: The team revised the plan; noun phrase: the team's revision of the plan.
- Keep the logical participants clear with possessives or appropriate prepositions.

## Examples in context

> The discovery of a second error delayed publication.
> Their refusal to negotiate surprised the committee.
> We decided to postpone the meeting rather than make a decision to postpone it.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

A longer noun phrase is not automatically better academic English. Do not assume a derived noun takes the same complement as its source verb: discuss the issue, but a discussion of/about the issue. Countability may change too: useful advice is uncountable, while a suggestion is countable. Prefer a clear active verb when an abstract noun would obscure responsibility.
