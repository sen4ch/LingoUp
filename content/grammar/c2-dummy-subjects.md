## Meaning and use

English clauses often require a grammatical subject even when no ordinary participant fills that role. Dummy it occurs in weather, time, distance, and extraposition patterns. Existential there introduces an entity or situation. Referential it instead points back to an identifiable thing or proposition. These forms can look similar on the surface while organising information differently. A clear analysis asks what supplies reference and what merely fills a grammatical position.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Weather/time: It is snowing. It is nearly noon.
- Extraposition: It matters that everyone agrees.
- Existence: There appears to be a problem.
- Referential it: I bought a lamp. It is blue.

## Examples in context

> It took us an hour to find the entrance.
> There have been several complaints this week.
> It is the old bridge that needs repair.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not replace existential there with it simply because both come before be. There followed by a linking or raising expression can still introduce existence: there seems to be. Agreement in careful existential writing generally follows the introduced noun phrase, though informal patterns vary. Extraposition is not identical to an it-cleft: the latter highlights a focused constituent.
