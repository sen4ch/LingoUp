## Meaning and use

A cleft divides a message to give special focus to one part. An it-cleft uses it + be + focused element followed by a clause. A wh-cleft uses a wh-clause, often beginning with what, and a form of be. These structures help correct an assumption, contrast alternatives, or organise information that the listener partly knows. They should serve an information purpose rather than simply lengthen every sentence.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- It was Maya who organised the exhibition.
- It was on Friday that we signed the agreement.
- What I need is a little more time.
- A reversed wh-cleft: A little more time is what I need.

## Examples in context

> It was the final paragraph that changed my opinion.
> What surprised us was the lack of any explanation.
> The person I wanted to speak to was the manager.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Keep the clause after the focus structurally complete apart from the element being focused. Do not add a duplicate object: It was this report that I read, not that I read it. An it-cleft normally uses that for a time or reason focus, not when or why in every case. Different agreement choices can occur in wh-clefts, so avoid overgeneralising from one example.
