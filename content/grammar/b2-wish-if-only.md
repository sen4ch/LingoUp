## Meaning and use

Wish and if only can express dissatisfaction with a present situation or regret about the past. A past form after wish commonly describes a different present, while a past perfect looks back at an unreal alternative to a past event. Wish plus would often focuses on a desired change in someone's behaviour or an unwelcome recurring situation. Hope is the normal choice for a future event you consider realistically possible.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Present alternative: I wish I knew the answer.
- Past regret: If only I had checked the address.
- Desired change: I wish they would speak more quietly.
- Ability: I wish I could stay longer.

## Examples in context

> I wish this room were warmer.
> She wishes she had taken more photographs.
> I hope your interview goes well tomorrow.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not use wish plus would as a general replacement for every unreal present state. I wish I were taller is different from a request for behaviour to change. In ordinary standard regret, prefer I wish I had listened to I wish I would have listened. Wish can also take a to infinitive in formal language: I wish to make a complaint.
