## Meaning and use

The past continuous presents an activity as being in progress at a past time. It can provide a background scene, describe simultaneous activities, or show an activity interrupted by a shorter event. Use the past simple to present a complete event in the story and the continuous to show its ongoing background. Neither while nor when determines the tense by itself; the intended relationship matters.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Was/were + verb-ing: I was reading; they were talking.
- Negative: was/were not + verb-ing.
- Question: was/were + subject + verb-ing?
- Background + event: I was cooking when the phone rang.

## Examples in context

> At eight yesterday, we were crossing the bridge.
> While I was reading, my brother was drawing.
> The lights went out while we were having dinner.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not confuse was working with worked: the first views an activity from inside, while the second can present the whole event. State verbs are often simple even in a background description: I knew the address. The continuous does not automatically mean an action was interrupted; it may simply locate the viewpoint.
