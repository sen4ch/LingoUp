## Meaning and use

Grammar interacts with what the reader already knows and what the speaker wants to highlight. Familiar information often appears earlier, while new or contrastive information appears later. End weight places a long or complex constituent towards the end when the construction allows it. Passive voice, extraposition, clefts, and alternative object patterns are tools for organising information, not interchangeable ways of making a sentence longer.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Given-to-new flow: connect the next subject to a familiar referent.
- Extraposition: It surprised us that the road was still open.
- Heavy object shift can place a long object after a shorter adjunct.
- Choose active or passive according to topic continuity and the importance of the agent.

## Examples in context

> A new bridge opened yesterday. The bridge connects two villages.
> It is difficult to explain why the figures changed so suddenly.
> We gave the visitor a map; we gave a map to every visitor who requested one.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

End weight is a tendency, not permission to rearrange any sentence. A pronoun object normally resists positions that are possible for a long noun phrase. Do not use passive voice merely because the agent is a person, or active voice merely because it is shorter. The best choice preserves both the intended meaning and a clear information path.
