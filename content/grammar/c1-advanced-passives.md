## Meaning and use

Passive constructions can involve several auxiliaries, two-object verbs, prepositional verbs, or an infinitival complement. Each auxiliary contributes tense, aspect, modality, or voice. The final participle normally carries the passive lexical meaning. Choosing a passive may also change what can be omitted and which participant is foregrounded. Some combinations are grammatical but stylistically cumbersome, so clarity can justify an active alternative.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Modal perfect passive: must have been checked.
- Two-object alternatives: She was offered a place; a place was offered to her.
- Prepositional passive: The children were well looked after.
- Passive make: They were made to wait.

## Examples in context

> The documents should have been delivered yesterday.
> I was given a replacement ticket at the desk.
> The missing funds are being looked into.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Retain a required preposition when it belongs to the verb: The problem was dealt with. Some passives cannot be formed just by moving any object-like phrase to the front. Long combinations such as will have been being repaired are rare and usually better rephrased. A get-passive may suggest change or personal involvement, but it is not a simple substitute in every context.
