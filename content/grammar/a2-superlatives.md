## Meaning and use

A superlative identifies the member of a group with the highest or lowest degree of a quality. Short adjectives commonly take -est, while longer adjectives normally use most or least. The comparison group may be stated with in or of, or supplied by context. Equality is a different pattern: as ... as compares two things without saying one has a greater degree.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- The + adjective-est: the smallest, the biggest, the happiest.
- The most/least + longer adjective: the most reliable.
- Irregular: good/best, bad/worst.
- As + adjective/adverb + as; not as ... as.

## Examples in context

> This is the quietest room in the building.
> That was one of the most useful lessons.
> My bag is not as heavy as yours.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

After one of the most, use a plural countable noun: one of the most interesting books. A possessive can replace the: my best friend, not my the best friend. Do not combine most and -est. For a comparison between two named alternatives, a comparative is often the natural choice: the cheaper of the two.
