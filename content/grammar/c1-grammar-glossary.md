## Meaning and use

Grammar terminology helps explain patterns precisely. A word class describes a word's grammatical behaviour; a phrase groups words around a head; a clause organises a situation with a predicate and usually a subject. The same word can belong to different classes in different contexts. A term should help you analyse a sentence, not replace the need to understand its meaning. Different reference grammars sometimes use slightly different labels.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Word classes include noun, verb, adjective, adverb, determiner, pronoun, preposition and conjunction.
- Functions include subject, object, complement and adjunct.
- Finite verb phrases carry tense or modal finiteness; non-finite phrases include infinitival and participial constructions.
- Tense, aspect, voice and mood describe different grammatical dimensions.

## Examples in context

> In The small dog barked loudly, dog is the noun-phrase head.
> In Maya became a doctor, a doctor is a subject complement, not a direct object.
> In We left after lunch, after lunch is a time adjunct.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not equate class with function: a noun phrase can be a subject, object, or complement. A subject is not always an agent, especially in passives. A phrase need not contain several words; a one-word noun phrase is possible. Some grammars classify determiners or non-finite clauses differently, so compare definitions rather than treating a change of label as a contradiction.

## Terms to look up

- **Noun**: A word class used to name entities, substances, events, and abstractions.
- **Verb**: A word class central to predicates, with finite or non-finite forms.
- **Adjective**: A word class commonly describing or classifying a noun or subject.
- **Adverb**: A class modifying a verb phrase, adjective, adverb, or wider clause.
- **Determiner**: An item helping specify the reference or quantity of a noun phrase.
- **Pronoun**: An item that can stand as or within a referring expression without a full lexical noun phrase.
- **Preposition**: An item heading a phrase that relates its complement to other material.
- **Conjunction**: A linking word introducing coordination or certain subordinate relationships.
- **Head**: The central element whose properties help determine a phrase’s type and distribution.
- **Subject**: A clause function associated with agreement and characteristic positions; not always an agent.
- **Object**: A complement selected by a verb or, in some descriptions, by a preposition.
- **Complement**: Material licensed or selected by a head, or a description/identification after a linking verb.
- **Adjunct**: An optional modifier adding circumstances such as time, reason, or manner.
- **Predicate**: The part of a clause that says something about its subject, commonly organised around a verb phrase.
- **Main clause**: A clause not functioning as a dependent part of another clause in that construction.
- **Subordinate clause**: A clause embedded in or dependent on a larger construction.
- **Finite**: Carrying tense or modal finiteness, rather than an independent bare infinitival or participial form.
- **Infinitive**: A non-finite verb form, used with to in some patterns and without to in others.
- **Participle**: An -ing or past-participle form used in verb combinations and non-finite constructions.
- **Auxiliary**: A helping verb contributing grammatical functions such as perfect aspect, passive voice, or question formation.
- **Modal**: An auxiliary such as can, must, or might expressing meanings including ability, possibility, or necessity.
- **Tense**: A grammatical distinction associated with time reference and, in some contexts, remoteness.
- **Aspect**: The grammatical presentation of a situation, such as continuous or perfect viewpoint.
- **Voice**: The organisation of participants in constructions such as active and passive.
- **Mood**: A category or constructional distinction connected with assertion, command, demand, or hypothetical meaning.
- **Transitive**: A verb use licensing an object; the same lexical verb may have other uses.
- **Intransitive**: A verb use without a direct object.
- **Valency**: The number and types of complements a head licenses.
- **Agreement**: Correspondence in features such as person or number between related elements.
- **Antecedent**: An expression supplying reference for a later pronoun or relative construction.
- **Reference**: The relationship between an expression and what it identifies in a context.
- **Scope**: The stretch of meaning affected by an operator such as not, only, or every.
- **Ellipsis**: Omission of material recoverable from the linguistic or situational context.
- **Inflection**: A grammatical change of word form, such as work/works or child/children.
- **Derivation**: Word formation producing a new lexical item, such as decide/decision.
- **Register**: A pattern of language choices associated with a situation, genre, audience, or purpose.
- **Dialect**: A language variety with systematic grammatical, lexical, and pronunciation patterns.
