## Meaning and use

The past perfect places a situation before an already established past viewpoint. It is especially useful when a narrative moves backwards to explain an earlier event, a result, or background knowledge. The form is had plus a past participle for every subject. Do not use it merely because an event happened a long time ago; it needs a relationship to a past reference point.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Had + past participle: she had left.
- Negative: had not/hadn't + past participle.
- Question: had + subject + past participle?
- After the earlier sequence is clear, a narrative can return to the past simple.

## Examples in context

> When we arrived, the film had already started.
> I recognised the street because I had lived there as a child.
> She could not log in because she had forgotten her password.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

The past perfect is not compulsory whenever before or after already makes the order clear. I left after he arrived can be natural. Compare When I arrived, she left with When I arrived, she had left: in the second sentence, her departure happened before the arrival. Do not confuse had as an auxiliary with ordinary past had in I had a bicycle.
