## Meaning and use

Prepositions connect a noun phrase to a place or spatial relationship. **In** commonly presents a place as an enclosed area, **on** as a surface, and **at** as a point or location for an activity. These are starting points, not mechanical rules for every expression. Other prepositions show relative position: under, above, behind, in front of, between, opposite, and next to. A place phrase can follow be or add information to an action.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- In a room, in a city, in a box; on a shelf, on a wall.
- At the station, at work, at home; next to the window.
- Between two points or groups; among members of a group.
- Ask Where is it? and answer with a complete place phrase.

## Examples in context

> Your glasses are on the bedside table.
> We are waiting at the bus stop.
> The bank is between the bakery and the cinema.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Say at home, not at the home when you mean someone’s own home in the ordinary expression. **In front of** and **opposite** are not identical: opposite means facing or across from something. On a bus and in a car are conventional transport expressions. Do not translate each preposition separately from another language; learn the whole phrase with its situation.
