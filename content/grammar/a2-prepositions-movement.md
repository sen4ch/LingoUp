## Meaning and use

Movement prepositions describe a destination, a route, or a change of position. To normally points to a destination, into to movement from outside to inside, and out of to the reverse. Across describes movement from one side to another over an area or surface; through describes a route within an enclosed or surrounding space. Towards indicates direction without necessarily implying arrival.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Destination: go to the library; but go home without to.
- Change of position: walk into the room, climb onto the roof.
- Route: across the square, through the tunnel, along the river.
- Direction: towards the exit; movement away: away from the fire.

## Examples in context

> We drove through a long tunnel.
> She walked across the bridge.
> The child ran towards the playground.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Distinguish location from movement: in the room versus into the room. Into is a preposition; in to can occur when in belongs to the verb and to begins the next phrase. Arrive normally takes at a point or in a city/country, not to: arrive at the station. Reach takes a direct object without a movement preposition.
