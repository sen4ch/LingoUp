## Meaning and use

Quantifiers express an amount or number. Their choice depends on countability, sentence type, and the meaning you intend. Many and few go with plural countable nouns; much and little usually go with uncountable nouns. Some often appears in positive statements, while any is common in negatives and questions. Some is also natural in offers and requests when a positive answer is expected.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Many/few/a few books; much/little/a little time.
- A lot of and lots of work with both plural and uncountable nouns.
- Some information; any questions; not any money.
- No + noun gives a negative meaning without another ordinary negative verb.

## Examples in context

> We have a few minutes before the meeting.
> There is little hope of finishing today.
> Would you like some tea?

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

A few and a little mean some; few and little often emphasise an amount that is small or insufficient. Much is less common in ordinary positive informal statements than a lot of, though too much and so much are normal. Any can mean whichever or no restriction in positive statements: Choose any seat. Avoid standard double negatives such as I do not have no money.
