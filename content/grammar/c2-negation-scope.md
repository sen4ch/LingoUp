## Meaning and use

The position and scope of a negative determine which part of a proposition is denied. Not all does not normally mean none, and not necessarily does not mean necessarily not. Negative-polarity items such as any and ever are common in negative clauses and certain questions or conditions. English also permits lexical negatives, restrictive words, and rhetorical negatives whose interpretation depends on context and register.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Not every candidate passed: universal success is denied.
- No candidate passed: success is denied for all candidates.
- She did not promise to leave differs from She promised not to leave.
- Hardly, scarcely, and rarely can create negative-like environments.

## Examples in context

> The solution is not necessarily the cheapest one.
> We hardly ever use that entrance.
> I did not say he had stolen it; I said the record was missing.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not collapse different scopes into the same translation. Not unhappy can be a restrained positive evaluation rather than full enthusiasm. Double negation in some dialects functions as negative concord; edited standard English normally uses a single negative pattern for the same meaning. A grammatical negative can still be pragmatically polite, rhetorical, or ironic, so separate structure from speaker intention.
