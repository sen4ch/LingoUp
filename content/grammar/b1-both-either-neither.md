## Meaning and use

These words describe whole groups, alternatives, or the absence of group members. Both refers to two together; either can refer to one or the other; neither excludes both alternatives. All usually refers to an entire group or amount. None means not any. Their patterns change depending on whether they precede a noun, an of phrase, or stand alone. Read the number and meaning rather than treating all these words as interchangeable.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Both books / both of the books; both of them.
- Either book / either of the books; neither option.
- All the students / all of them; none of the money.
- Both ... and; either ... or; neither ... nor.

## Examples in context

> Both of my parents work nearby.
> Neither answer is correct.
> None of the water is safe to drink.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Either and neither normally take a singular countable noun directly: either route, not either routes. Agreement after neither of and none of varies with register and intended number; singular neither of is a useful formal pattern. Both ... and normally makes a plural subject. Do not add another negative to neither in an ordinary standard sentence.
