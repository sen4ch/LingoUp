## Meaning and use

English verbs have a small set of forms that combine with auxiliaries. The base form appears after central modals and do; the third-person singular is used in the positive present simple; the past form appears in the past simple; a past participle follows have in perfect forms and be in passives; the -ing form follows be in continuous forms. Irregular verbs may use different past and participle forms.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Regular: work, works, worked, worked, working.
- Irregular: go, goes, went, gone, going.
- After did/can/will: base form, not a past form.
- Have + past participle; be + -ing for an ongoing activity.

## Examples in context

> They went home after the concert.
> They have gone home, so the house is empty.
> Did she take the last bus?

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not confuse a past form with a past participle: saw/seen, took/taken, wrote/written. An -ing ending alone does not make a complete finite verb: She is working needs is. Learn irregular verbs in groups or in example sentences, not only as isolated lists. British and American forms sometimes differ, such as learnt/learned.
