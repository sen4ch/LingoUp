## Meaning and use

A question can do more than ask for facts: it can request an action, offer help, or ask permission. Can, could, may, will, and would have different degrees of formality and politeness depending on context. Could can refer to a present request, not only a past ability. Make the purpose clear by choosing the subject: Could you asks another person to act; Could I asks about an action by the speaker.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Request: Could you + base verb? Would you mind + -ing?
- Permission: Can/Could/May I + base verb?
- Offer: Shall I + base verb? Would you like + noun/to infinitive?
- Polite invitation: Would you like to join us?

## Examples in context

> Could you check the address, please?
> May I use this chair?
> Would you mind closing the door?

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Would you mind is followed by -ing, not a to infinitive. A literal No, not at all response to Do you mind means there is no objection, so interpret the whole reply. Politeness depends on tone, relationship, and situation, not a single magic word. Could I does not force a past-time meaning.
