## Meaning and use

Grammar helps distinguish a fact, an inference, a possibility, and a personal evaluation. Modal verbs, stance adverbs, reporting expressions, and introductory it clauses can reduce or increase commitment to a claim. Hedging is useful when evidence is limited, but vague language should not conceal an unsupported assertion. A precise qualifier identifies the boundary of a claim more clearly than an accumulation of weak words.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Possibility: may/might/could; tendency: tends to, is likely to.
- Evidence-based attribution: appears to, is reported to.
- Stance adverbs: apparently, probably, arguably.
- It seems that / There is some evidence that + clause.

## Examples in context

> The change may have improved access for some users.
> These findings appear to support the earlier explanation.
> It is likely that the delay will affect the timetable.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

May is not the same as will, and apparently does not automatically mean certainly. Distinguish reporting another person's claim from endorsing it as fact. Avoid contradictory combinations such as definitely might when a precise level of confidence is needed. A passive report also requires a reliable source in real communication; grammar alone cannot provide evidence.
