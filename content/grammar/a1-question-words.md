## Meaning and use

Question words ask for a specific kind of information. Who asks about a person, where about a place, when about time, why about a reason, and how about a manner or condition. What and which ask for identification; which often suggests a restricted choice. In many questions, the auxiliary comes before the subject. A question word can also be the subject, in which case the ordinary positive order remains.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Question word + be + subject: Where is your office?
- Question word + do/does + subject + base verb: What do you need?
- Subject question: Who lives here?
- How + adjective/adverb: How old? How often? How far?

## Examples in context

> Who teaches your evening class?
> Where does your cousin live?
> Which of these two jackets do you prefer?

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Distinguish **Who called Maya?** from **Who did Maya call?** The first asks for the subject; the second asks for the object. Do not add does in a positive subject question: Who knows the answer? Why normally needs a reason, often beginning with because. How many goes with a plural countable noun, while how much goes with an uncountable noun or a price.
