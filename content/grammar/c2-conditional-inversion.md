## Meaning and use

Formal conditionals can omit if and invert certain auxiliary forms. Had introduces a past-perfect counterfactual, were can introduce an unreal state or a were-to construction, and should can present a tentative future possibility. Conditions can also be implied by an imperative, a prepositional phrase, or a phrase such as otherwise. Interpret the relationship between propositions, not just the presence of if.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Had + subject + participle: Had we known, we would have acted.
- Were + subject + to infinitive: Were the proposal to fail, we would reconsider.
- Should + subject + base verb: Should you need help, contact us.
- Without/but for + noun phrase can establish a hypothetical condition.

## Examples in context

> Were I in your position, I would ask for clarification.
> Should the flight be cancelled, we will notify you.
> Without your advice, I would have made a costly mistake.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not invert an ordinary past-simple verb by simply moving it before the subject. A negative inverted condition normally puts not after the subject: Had she not called. But for usually means if it were not for or if it had not been for, with the time supplied by context. Formal inversion is a register choice, not a requirement for an advanced sentence.
