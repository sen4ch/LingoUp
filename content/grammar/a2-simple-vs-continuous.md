## Meaning and use

Choose a present form by the way you present a situation. The **simple** commonly presents a routine, a stable fact, or a state; the **continuous** presents an activity in progress, a temporary arrangement, or a changing situation. The same verb can allow different viewpoints. Time expressions help, but they are not automatic tense selectors. Living somewhere can be described as relatively stable or as a temporary experience.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Routine: I work in a cafe; current activity: I am working on a report.
- Stable situation: She lives here; temporary situation: She is living with us.
- State: I believe you; activity meaning: I am tasting the soup.
- Repeated irritation can use always + continuous: He is always losing his keys.

## Examples in context

> We usually take the bus, but today we are walking.
> I think this route is faster.
> The chef is tasting the sauce before serving it.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not treat every present-time action as continuous. Stative meanings such as own, belong, know, and need normally use simple forms. Some verbs have both state and activity meanings: have a car, but have lunch; see what you mean, but see a doctor tomorrow. Use the meaning of the whole sentence when choosing.
