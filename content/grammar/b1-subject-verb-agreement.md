## Meaning and use

A finite verb normally agrees with the grammatical subject, not with a nearby noun inside a modifying phrase. This is especially important with long subjects, indefinite pronouns, and expressions containing of. Most of the present-simple distinction appears in the third-person singular; be has more agreement forms. Number and meaning interact in some collective and quantity expressions, which are developed further in advanced lessons.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- The key to the cupboards is missing: key is the subject head.
- Everyone, somebody, and each commonly take singular agreement.
- Two subjects joined by and normally take a plural verb.
- A number of people are; the number of people is.

## Examples in context

> Each of the rooms has a balcony.
> The books on the top shelf belong to Jo.
> The number of applications is increasing.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not let a plural noun in an of phrase control the verb when the head is singular. News is normally singular despite its ending; scissors and trousers normally use plural agreement. Collective nouns can allow singular or plural agreement depending on dialect and whether the group is viewed as a unit or its members. Consistency and the intended meaning matter.
