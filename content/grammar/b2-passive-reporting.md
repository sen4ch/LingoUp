## Meaning and use

Reporting passives present a claim without naming its source in the subject position. They are common in news and formal reports. One pattern uses it plus a passive reporting verb and a that clause. Another makes the person or thing being discussed the subject and follows the passive verb with an infinitive. The infinitive can show whether the reported situation is simultaneous, ongoing, or earlier than the reporting viewpoint.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- It is believed that the painting is genuine.
- The painting is believed to be genuine.
- Earlier event: She is reported to have left.
- Ongoing event: They are thought to be negotiating.

## Examples in context

> It is expected that the road will reopen tomorrow.
> The missing hikers are believed to be safe.
> The company is said to have sold its oldest factory.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

A passive report attributes information; it does not prove that the claim is true. Use a perfect infinitive for an earlier event when needed: to have arrived, not to arrived. Not every reporting verb accepts every pattern. Avoid using an impersonal construction to hide a source when the source matters to the reliability of a real claim.
