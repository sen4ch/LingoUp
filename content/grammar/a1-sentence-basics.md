## Meaning and use

A complete statement normally has a **subject** and a **verb**. The subject identifies who or what the clause is about; the verb expresses an action or state. A verb may be followed by an object, a description, or information about a place. English usually puts the subject before the verb in ordinary statements. A clause can be a complete sentence, or it can join another clause. Learn the basic patterns before adding longer descriptions. A sentence begins with a capital letter and ends with a full stop, a question mark, or an exclamation mark.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Subject + verb: Birds sing.
- Subject + verb + object: Maya reads novels.
- Subject + be + description: The room is quiet.
- Subject + verb + place: We live in Bristol.

## Examples in context

> My neighbour repairs bicycles.
> The new classroom is upstairs.
> We study English after dinner.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not omit the subject in an ordinary statement: say **It is cold**, not Is cold. English adjectives do not replace the verb: say **She is happy**, not She happy. Instructions are an exception: Open the window has an understood subject, you. Keep an adverb between the auxiliary and main verb when the normal pattern requires it, rather than separating a verb from a short object.
