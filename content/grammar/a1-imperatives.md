## Meaning and use

An imperative gives an instruction, invitation, warning, or request. It normally begins with the base form of the verb, without an expressed subject. The implied listener is you. A negative imperative begins with don’t. Tone and context matter: a bare instruction may sound natural in a recipe but abrupt in a request to a stranger. Please can soften a request, while let’s proposes an action for the speaker and listener together.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Base verb + object or other information: Close the door.
- Do not/don’t + base verb: Do not touch the screen.
- Be in an instruction: Be careful. Don’t be late.
- Let’s + base verb; let’s not + base verb.

## Examples in context

> Turn left after the traffic lights.
> Please put your name at the top.
> Let’s take a short break.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not use to at the start of a normal instruction: Open your books, not To open your books. Negative be instructions need don’t: Don’t be noisy. Let’s means let us in a suggestion; lets without an apostrophe is a different verb form. For a more polite request, compare Could you open the window? rather than only adding more exclamation marks.
