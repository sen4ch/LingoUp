## Meaning and use

Many verbs, adjectives, and nouns select particular prepositions. These patterns are not always predictable from spatial meanings. Their choice can change with the intended sense or with the part of speech: depend on, dependent on, but dependence on; discuss a subject, but a discussion about/of it. Learn the entire complement pattern and note whether a person, thing, noun phrase, or -ing clause follows.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Verb patterns: depend on, object to, apologise to someone for something.
- Adjective patterns: interested in, responsible for, capable of.
- Noun patterns: access to, an increase in, a solution to.
- After a preposition, a verb commonly takes -ing: object to paying.

## Examples in context

> She apologised to the customer for the delay.
> We are responsible for checking the results.
> They objected to being recorded.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Not every to introduces an infinitive: look forward to hearing, be committed to improving, and object to paying contain a preposition. Avoid adding a preposition to a verb that normally takes a direct object: discuss the issue, enter the room, approach the desk. Some alternatives are legitimate but change meaning: agree with a person, agree to a proposal, agree on a plan.
