## Meaning and use

Standard English includes recognised regional alternatives. Differences include preferences in recent-event tense choice, collective-noun agreement, prepositions, and some verb forms. Most of the grammar is shared. Choose a consistent variety when a task specifies one, but do not treat every established alternative as a learner error. Individual speakers and contexts vary within each variety.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Recent events: British English often favours present perfect with just/already/yet; American past simple is also common.
- Collective nouns can more readily take plural agreement in British usage.
- Possession: have got is common in British conversation; have is widespread in both.
- Participles include got/gotten and learnt/learned with differences of meaning and preference.

## Examples in context

> At the weekend and on the weekend are established regional patterns.
> The team is winning presents the team as a unit.
> The team are discussing their positions can focus on members in British usage.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Avoid claiming that Americans never use present perfect or British speakers never use learned. Gotten is not a simple replacement for every got: have got for possession differs from have gotten for acquisition in common American usage. A style guide may prefer one form without making another regional form ungrammatical. Meanings still matter when deciding whether two sentences are genuinely equivalent.
