## Meaning and use

Grammatical choices depend on genre, audience, relationship, and purpose. Conversation often tolerates recoverable fragments, left-dislocation, contractions, and response tokens. Formal edited prose usually makes relationships more explicit and avoids unexplained fragments. Neither style is inherently more intelligent or expressive. A speaker can shift register deliberately without changing the underlying factual claim.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Conversational ellipsis: Seen my keys? Need any help?
- Left-dislocation: That new cafe, it closes early.
- Formal alternatives often use fuller clauses and more explicit links.
- Absolute clause: Weather permitting, we will leave at dawn.

## Examples in context

> Want another cup? is natural in an informal exchange.
> The new cafe closes early is a neutral written alternative.
> All things considered, the shorter route is safer.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not mark every conversational fragment as a random error. It must still be recoverable and appropriate to the context. An absolute construction has its own expressed subject, unlike an ordinary shared-subject participle clause. Register variation does not excuse accidental ambiguity in a contract or safety instruction. Adapt the structure to the task, rather than inserting formal vocabulary into an otherwise unclear sentence.
