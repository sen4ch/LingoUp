## Meaning and use

English uses several forms to talk about the future. Will often makes a prediction, an offer, a promise, or a decision made as the speaker talks. Be going to often expresses an existing intention or a prediction based on present evidence. The present continuous commonly presents a personal arrangement, while the present simple can describe a timetable. These meanings overlap; the context and the speaker’s viewpoint are more important than rigid labels.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Will + base verb: I will help you.
- Am/is/are going to + base verb: She is going to study law.
- Present continuous arrangement: We are meeting at six.
- Present simple timetable: The flight leaves at noon.

## Examples in context

> The phone is ringing. I will answer it.
> Those clouds are very dark. It is going to rain.
> We are having dinner with Ana tomorrow.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not use will to after a modal: will help, not will to help. Going to needs be: They are going to leave. Arrangements often include a time or partner, but do not assume every planned future must be continuous. In most future time clauses, use a present form: I will call when I arrive, not when I will arrive.
