## Meaning and use

The future continuous presents an activity as in progress at a future viewpoint. The future perfect looks back from a future point at something completed earlier. The future perfect continuous emphasises duration up to that future point. These forms are useful for schedules, predictions, and expectations, but they do not guarantee that an event will occur. Choose the form that matches whether you emphasise an ongoing activity, completion, or duration.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Will be + -ing: I will be travelling at noon.
- Will have + past participle: We will have finished by noon.
- Will have been + -ing: By June, she will have been teaching for ten years.
- By marks a deadline or viewpoint; until marks continuation to an endpoint.

## Examples in context

> This time tomorrow, we will be flying over the coast.
> By Friday, I will have sent all the invitations.
> By nine, they will have been waiting for two hours.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not omit be in the future continuous or have in the future perfect. Compare will finish by Friday with will have finished by Friday: both can refer to completion, but the perfect explicitly looks back from the future viewpoint. State verbs usually avoid the continuous even when a duration is given: will have known each other for ten years.
