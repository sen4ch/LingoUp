## Meaning and use

Relative constructions can place a preposition before the relative word or leave it later in the clause. Fronted prepositions favour whom for people and which for things in formal style. Preposition stranding is common and grammatical in ordinary English. Quantified relatives identify a subset of a previously mentioned group. Supplementary clauses with which can also comment on an entire preceding proposition.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Formal: the person to whom I spoke; ordinary: the person I spoke to.
- Thing: the issue about which they argued / the issue they argued about.
- Quantified: three proposals, two of which were accepted.
- Possessive: a house whose roof was damaged; a house the roof of which was damaged.

## Examples in context

> We interviewed six candidates, all of whom had relevant experience.
> The research produced several findings, none of which were expected.
> This is the route along which the original railway ran.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not put that directly after a fronted preposition: the subject about which we spoke, not about that. Stranding a preposition is not inherently an error, but some fixed expressions restrict movement. Whose can refer to things as well as people. Avoid omitting an obligatory relative after a fronted preposition or in a standard non-defining clause.
