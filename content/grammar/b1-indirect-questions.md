## Meaning and use

An embedded question sits inside another clause. It may express uncertainty, report a question, or make a request more polite. Unlike a direct question, the embedded clause normally uses statement word order: subject before verb. Yes/no embedded questions often start with if or whether. A full sentence may still be a question because its main clause is interrogative, not because the embedded part reverses its subject and verb.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Direct: Where is the station? Embedded: Do you know where the station is?
- Direct do-support disappears: Where does she work? I wonder where she works.
- Yes/no: Can you tell me if/whether the shop is open?
- Subject questions keep their natural order: Do you know who called?

## Examples in context

> Could you tell me what this sign means?
> I do not know whether the train has left.
> Do you remember where we parked?

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not say Do you know where is the station? Only the main clause has question order. Whether is required in some patterns, including before a to infinitive: whether to stay, not if to stay. A statement such as I wonder where she lives normally ends with a full stop, not a question mark.
