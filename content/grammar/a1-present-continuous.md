## Meaning and use

Use the present continuous for an action happening now or around the present, a temporary situation, or a change in progress. Its form combines present be with an -ing form. It often contrasts with the present simple, which describes routines or stable facts. Context matters more than a single time word: a temporary course may last several months and still be described with the continuous. A future arrangement is another common use, developed in the future-forms lessons.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Positive: subject + am/is/are + verb-ing.
- Negative: subject + am/is/are not + verb-ing.
- Question: am/is/are + subject + verb-ing?
- Spelling: make/making, run/running, lie/lying; play/playing.

## Examples in context

> I am waiting for the next train.
> Our neighbours are staying with relatives this week.
> The days are getting shorter.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not omit be: She is reading, not She reading. Do not use a normal present ending after be: They are working, not They are work. Many state meanings normally use the simple form: I know the answer, not I am knowing the answer. Some verbs change meaning: I think you are right expresses an opinion; I am thinking about the problem describes a mental activity.
