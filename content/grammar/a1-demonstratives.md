## Meaning and use

Demonstratives point to things in space, time, or a conversation. **This/these** often signal something near the speaker or something currently being introduced. **That/those** often point further away or refer back to something already mentioned. Use this and that with singular nouns, and these and those with plural nouns. Demonstratives can stand before a noun or replace a noun phrase. The distinction is about the speaker’s viewpoint, so physical distance is not the only consideration.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Singular: this chair, that chair; plural: these chairs, those chairs.
- Without a noun: This is useful. Those are expensive.
- Introductions: This is my colleague, Sam.
- Phone identification: Hello, this is Priya speaking.

## Examples in context

> These shoes here are mine.
> That building across the river is a museum.
> We missed the train. That was frustrating.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not combine a singular demonstrative with a plural noun: these books, not this books. Use plural agreement with these/those: Those are mine. This and that can refer to a whole idea, not only a physical object. After a demonstrative, choose the correct noun number before choosing the verb.
