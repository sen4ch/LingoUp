## Meaning and use

Articles help the listener identify a noun. Use **a/an** with a singular countable noun when you are introducing one member of a group. Use **the** when the situation, an earlier mention, or a description makes the intended thing identifiable. Choose a or an by the first sound of the following word, not simply its first letter. A singular countable common noun normally needs an article or another determiner such as my or this. Plural and uncountable nouns can appear without an article when the meaning is general.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- A before a consonant sound: a book, a university.
- An before a vowel sound: an apple, an hour.
- First mention: a key. Identifiable later mention: the key.
- Other determiners replace the article: my bag, this bag.

## Examples in context

> I saw a fox. The fox ran behind a wall.
> She is an engineer at a university.
> We like music, but the music in this film is too loud.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not write an university: university begins with a y sound. Do not combine an article with a possessive determiner: my coat, not the my coat. Occupations normally take a/an: He is a nurse. General plural nouns do not normally take the: Dogs need exercise. More specialised article choices are covered in the articles reference lesson.
