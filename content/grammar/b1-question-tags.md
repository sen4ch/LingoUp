## Meaning and use

Question tags invite confirmation, check information, or maintain contact with a listener. A common pattern pairs a positive statement with a negative tag and a negative statement with a positive tag. The tag repeats the appropriate auxiliary and uses a subject pronoun. If the statement has no auxiliary, use do, does, or did. Intonation can distinguish a genuine check from an expectation of agreement.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- She is ready, isn't she? They have finished, haven't they?
- You work here, don't you? He left, didn't he?
- I'm late, aren't I? Let's go, shall we?
- Negative words such as never normally take a positive tag.

## Examples in context

> You have met the new manager, haven't you?
> Nobody called, did they?
> It never rains here in July, does it?

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Choose the auxiliary from the actual verb phrase, not only the nearest word. Standard conversational aren't I is the usual tag after I am. Someone and everyone commonly take they in a tag. Imperatives have several possible tags, including will you; these are not formed exactly like ordinary statement tags.
