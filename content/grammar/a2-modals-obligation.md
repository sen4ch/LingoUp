## Meaning and use

These forms express different kinds of obligation, necessity, and advice. Must often presents a strong requirement from the speaker or an authority; have to often reports a practical or external requirement. Should usually gives advice or an expectation. Need to states necessity. The distinction between must and have to is not absolute. Their negatives, however, have an important meaning difference: prohibition is not the same as absence of necessity.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Must/should + base verb; have/has to + base verb.
- Must not: prohibited. Do not have to: not necessary.
- Past obligation: had to. Future obligation: will have to.
- Advice: should/should not; necessity: need to/do not need to.

## Examples in context

> Passengers must keep the aisle clear.
> You do not have to bring lunch; it is provided.
> You should save a copy of the file.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not use must not when you mean optional. You must not leave means leaving is forbidden; you do not have to leave means you may stay. Must does not form an ordinary past tense for obligation; use had to. A modal takes no third-person ending: She must go, but She has to go.
