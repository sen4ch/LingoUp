## Meaning and use

After expressions of a demand, recommendation, or necessity, a that clause can use the plain verb form. This is often called a mandative subjunctive. The verb does not take a third-person -s ending, and be remains be for all subjects. A pattern with should is also common, especially in British English. This construction expresses what is proposed or required rather than simply reporting an existing fact.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- They recommended that she apply.
- It is essential that he be present.
- Negative: They insisted that he not be interrupted.
- Alternative: They suggested that we should wait.

## Examples in context

> The committee requested that the meeting be postponed.
> I recommend that every applicant read the instructions.
> It is vital that the files remain available.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Distinguish a demand from a factual claim: He insisted that she leave can demand departure; He insisted that she was innocent asserts a belief. Do not automatically turn every that clause into a subjunctive. Suggest can mean propose or indicate evidence. This pattern is not a new tense, and its form can remain plain after a past reporting verb.
