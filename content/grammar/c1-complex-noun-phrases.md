## Meaning and use

A noun phrase can contain determiners, adjectives, noun modifiers, prepositional phrases, and relative or non-finite clauses. Its head noun determines the central meaning and often controls agreement. A long phrase is easiest to interpret when modifiers attach unambiguously. Noun modifiers usually stay singular, although established compounds have exceptions. Different positions can change which word a modifier describes.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- Determiner + premodifiers + head + postmodifiers.
- The report on the two factories is ready: report is the head.
- A three-day course uses singular day inside the compound modifier.
- The people invited yesterday is a noun phrase, not a complete sentence.

## Examples in context

> The detailed report on air quality arrived yesterday.
> We booked a two-bedroom apartment near the station.
> A proposal to replace the oldest machines was approved.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Do not let the nearest plural modifier replace the singular head for agreement. Avoid long noun stacks when the relationship is unclear; a prepositional phrase or clause may be clearer. A five-year plan has no plural s in year, but five years of planning has a plural noun. Use punctuation to separate supplementary description when it does not define the intended referent.
