## Meaning and use

Indefinite pronouns refer to people, things, or places without specifying their identity. Forms with some- often appear in positive statements; any- is common in questions and negatives, and can also mean no restriction. Forms with no- already express a negative idea. Everyone, everything, and similar subjects normally take a singular verb even when they refer to many people or things.

## Forms and sentence patterns

- People: someone/somebody, anyone/anybody, no one/nobody, everyone/everybody.
- Things: something, anything, nothing, everything.
- Places: somewhere, anywhere, nowhere, everywhere.
- Adjective after the pronoun: something useful, somewhere quiet.

## Examples in context

> Someone has left a scarf on the chair.
> There is nothing unusual about the result.
> Everyone is welcome; they can bring a friend.

## Common mistakes and important distinctions

Use everyone is, not everyone are. Singular they can refer back to an indefinite person: Someone called, but they did not leave a name. Do not combine ordinary negative verbs with nobody or nothing in standard English. No one is written as two words. Say something interesting, not interesting something.
